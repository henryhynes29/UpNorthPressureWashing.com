Black Streaks & Moss on Your Roof: What's Actually Growing Up There

By the Up North Pressure Washing crew · Duluth, MN · Updated June 2026

Stand across the street from almost any 10-year-old house in Duluth and you'll see them: dark vertical streaks bleeding down the north slope of the roof. Most homeowners assume it's dirt, soot, or "just what old shingles look like." It's none of those. It's alive, it's eating your shingles, and on the wet side of your house it's probably brought friends.

Meet the Three Organisms on a Northland Roof

Our climate is ideal for all three: Lake Superior humidity, long damp springs, shaded lots full of mature pines, and shingle temperatures that stay cool. That's why a Duluth roof streaks years before the same roof would in Fargo.

What It Costs You to Ignore

Algae and moss shorten roof life from both ends. The organisms hold moisture against the mat through our 80+ annual freeze-thaw cycles, moss physically lifts the shingle edges that wind then catches, and darker streaked sections run hotter in summer, accelerating asphalt aging. Roofers in our area regularly see moss-side slopes fail 5–10 years before the rest of the roof.

The replacement math: a new asphalt roof on an average Northland home runs $10,000–$20,000+. A professional cleaning that resets the biology costs a few hundred dollars every several years. You don't need a spreadsheet for that one.

What never to do: pressure wash shingles (strips granules, voids warranties — GAF, Owens Corning, and the Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association all specify low-pressure cleaning only), dry-scrape lichen (tears the granule layer off with it), or walk a wet mossy roof (we wear harnesses for a reason).

The Fix That Works: Soft Washing

Soft washing applies a sodium-hypochlorite-based solution at garden-hose pressure — under 100 PSI. The chemistry kills all three organisms to the root structure, streaks disappear immediately or fade over days, and dead moss releases and sheds off with weather over the following weeks. No granule loss, no warranty problems, and treatment keeps regrowth away for years because it kills the colony rather than shaving its surface.

Prevention That Actually Works (and What Doesn't)

Works: trimming branches to get sun on problem slopes, keeping gutters clean so the roof edge dries (see our ice dam guide — same fall visit handles both), and zinc or copper strips at the ridge, which release growth-suppressing ions in rain — modest but real on the upper courses.

Mostly marketing: "algae-resistant" shingles help for the first several years while their copper granules are fresh, but Northland conditions overwhelm them eventually. And the DIY bleach-in-a-garden-sprayer videos? The chemistry is directionally right, but without proper mix ratios, surfactants, plant protection, and fall protection it's how homeowners end up with streaked siding, dead foundation plantings, or an ER visit.

FAQ

What are the black streaks on my roof?

Gloeocapsa magma — airborne algae feeding on the limestone filler in your shingles. The black is its UV shield. North and shaded slopes streak first.

Is it actually damaging anything?

Yes, slowly — and the moss it accompanies is worse, lifting shingle edges through freeze-thaw. Streaked slopes fail years early.

Can I just pressure wash it?

No — pressure strips granules and can void your shingle warranty. Low-pressure soft washing is the manufacturer-specified method.

How long does a cleaning last?

Several years typically — the treatment kills colonies to the root rather than shaving the surface. Sunnier roofs stay clean longer; deep-shade lots regrow faster. Our soft washes carry a 1-year regrowth guarantee.
