Orange Concrete: Find the Iron, Fix the Stain

By the Up North Pressure Washing crew · Duluth, MN · Updated June 2026

Orange-brown staining on concrete is iron oxide every time, but the iron arrives four different ways — and matching the fix to the source decides whether the stain stays gone or returns by August. The diagnostic is usually written right on the slab in the stain's shape.

Read the Shape, Find the Source

Why Your Cleaner Made It Worse

The most common DIY mistake on rust is reaching for bleach or a standard house-wash mix: hypochlorite oxidizes iron further — you're literally feeding the reaction, and the stain sets deeper and darker. Rust is a mineral stain, and it answers to acid-side chemistry: oxalic acid and dedicated rust removers (often oxalic/phosphoric blends) convert the iron oxide into soluble compounds that rinse away. Applied correctly — wet the slab, apply, controlled dwell, agitate, rinse thoroughly — even old irrigation staining releases impressively. Deep, repeated soak-ins may take rounds, and ancient stains can ghost like old oil, but rust is one of the more winnable stains in the book.

Handle with respect: oxalic chemistry needs gloves and eye protection, kills moss and plants it puddles on, and etches some decorative finishes — test patches first on stamped or colored concrete. This is also a stain category where the pro visit is cheap relative to the experimentation.

Keep It Gone

The cleaning is the easy half; the iron source is the assignment. Sprinkler heads re-aimed, slabs swept after fertilizing, pads under metal feet, and a penetrating sealer on the clean slab so the next iron delivery sits on the surface instead of soaking in. Houses on iron-rich well water fighting the orange everywhere — driveway, siding splash zones, even window glass — are usually one iron filter away from ending the whole war.

FAQ

What causes orange stains on my driveway?

Iron — from sprinklers on well water, fertilizer granules, rusting metal contact, or rebar bleed. The pattern tells you which.

Does bleach remove rust stains from concrete?

No — bleach feeds the reaction. Rust needs oxalic-based removers, not chlorine.

Can irrigation rust stains be removed?

Yes, with proper rust chemistry — then re-aim the sprinklers or it’s back by August.
