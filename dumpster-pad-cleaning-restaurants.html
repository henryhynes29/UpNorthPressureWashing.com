The Dumpster Pad: Your Restaurant’s Most Honest Square Footage

By the Up North Pressure Washing crew · Duluth, MN · Updated June 2026

Ask a health inspector or a pest-control tech where they look first behind a restaurant and you'll get the same answer: the dumpster pad. It's the most honest square footage on the property — it shows exactly how the operation actually runs when no customer is watching. It's also a solvable problem on a cheap cadence, which makes the neglected version purely self-inflicted.

What's Actually Happening on That Concrete

Every bag drip, bin leak, and grease-cart transfer deposits animal fats and food liquids into hot summer concrete — which absorbs them like the sponge it is. The fats turn rancid in the pores (that smell isn't the dumpster — it's the pad), the film becomes genuinely slippery when wet (your staff carries trash across it daily, and slip claims behind restaurants are a real category), and the whole zone becomes a scent beacon and food source for the pest pressure your contract tech keeps writing up. Flies breed in the grease film itself; rodents patrol the perimeter. A pad can be dirtier than the inside of the dumpster on it.

Why the Mop-and-Hose Approach Loses

Cold water moves surface grease around and pushes it into the pores; degreaser without hot water lifts a fraction; and the slick layer hiding in the broom-finish texture survives it all. What works is the commercial protocol: alkaline degreaser with dwell, hot-water surface cleaning (heat is non-negotiable on fats — same chemistry as your dish pit), and managed rinse-down. Done on cadence, pads stay at "boring" — which is the goal state.

The wastewater rule most operators don’t know: grease-laden washwater generally can’t just be hosed to the storm drain — storm systems run to lakes and streams, not treatment, and municipalities can cite for it. Professional pad cleaning includes wash-water management (containment, recovery, or sanitary-drain routing per local rules). It’s a real compliance difference between a pro service and a kid with a pressure washer — and it protects the lake we all live on.

FAQ

How often should a restaurant dumpster pad be cleaned?

Busy kitchens monthly; most restaurants every 1–2 months in season plus a spring opener.

Why does my dumpster area smell even after the dumpster is emptied?

The pad itself — rancid fats in the concrete pores. Hot-water degreasing fixes what hosing can’t.

Can grease washwater go down the storm drain?

No — storm drains feed the lake. Pro service manages the washwater; that’s part of what you’re buying.
